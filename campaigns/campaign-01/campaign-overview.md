# Campaign Overview

## The Anomaly

Something happened.

Not a war. Not a meteor. Not a virus.

An electrical anomaly rolled across the planet — a galaxy-scale surge that bled through satellites, power grids, aircraft, data centres, cars, phones… people. For a few impossible minutes, the sky shimmered like heat haze and every powered thing on Earth screamed.

Then most of it died.

Some cities went dark and never came back. Some grids flicker back to life in patches. Vehicles work if they're old enough. Lights sometimes turn on. Nothing with circuitry works — but basic electrical components do. Someone who knows how to build circuits from scratch can build something new.

Governments fractured. Corporations vanished. Neighbourhoods became territories. The world didn't end — it just stalled. And something else started.

### Waking Up

The anomaly affected people like a switch being thrown. Physiology simply stopped — no dreams, no decay, no awareness of time passing. Waking up feels like waking from a deep, good sleep.

Wake times varied enormously. Some survivors woke within a week. Others took months. The party woke approximately 2 years after the anomaly — the latest known cases. Most people have no explanation for the variation. **The truth is that Charlotte's proximity to the party extended their dormancy** — she survived the surge inside her parents' car (a partial Faraday cage), and the dampening effect radiated outward. She has carried this without understanding it. The party don't know this yet. The reveal, when it comes, recontextualises how they found her.

Charlotte has been following the party discretely since they left Brad's house. She eventually works up the courage to knock on the community centre door.

### The Cause

The anomaly was not a natural occurrence. **It was caused deliberately by a single individual** — the campaign's true final villain. Their research was into the modification of human physiology through bioelectric stimulation. The anomaly was an experiment that malfunctioned at a catastrophic scale. The event diminished this person significantly — whatever they built to cause it was partially destroyed — but they are still alive, still operating, still working toward their original goal.

This truth will not be revealed until the final arcs. Breadcrumbs should be planted from Arc 1 onward.

### Powers

The anomaly interacted with the bioelectricity already present in every human body. In some people, something was unlocked or enhanced — latent capacity that the surge activated. Not everyone changed. No one fully understands why.

Charged people tending crops tend to yield more, faster — a passive effect, not a specific ability. Useful for feeding communities.

### Spell Scraps

Spell scraps are encoded clinical documents — shorthand instructions for stimulating specific bioelectric pathways, produced by Dr. Maren Voss's pre-anomaly research facility. They were not designed as "spells." They were lab documentation, encoded symbolically so they could be distributed without being understood by the wrong people.

The research facility still exists, somewhere in or near the city. It is partially destroyed, partially intact. It will be the location of the campaign's final confrontation.

The scraps were scattered across the city by the anomaly event itself — in ruins, on bodies, in pockets of the dead, hidden in books. The community centre old man who gave the party their first batch found his years ago and had no idea what they were.

Charlotte can perceive the symbols in her sleep and draw them from memory. She does not have powers. She was neurologically primed by her exposure during the anomaly — the Faraday cage effect worked both ways. She will at some point attempt to use a scrap herself. It backfires — she starts burning, screaming, until the party stop her. Not fatal. Deeply upsetting. A strong attachment moment before the gut punch.

## Group Origin

On the night of the anomaly, the party were all together at Brad's house — a backyard gathering, nothing heroic. Music too loud. Too many drinks. Not all of them knew each other well.

When the surge hit, everything blew at once. The air tasted like copper. The sky flashed white. Everyone blacked out.

When they woke — roughly 2 years later — Brad was gone. Not even a trace.

Charlotte had been nearby. Her parents died in the street during the surge. She survived in their car. She saw the party unconscious at Brad's house and has been quietly watching over them, or at least keeping them in sight, ever since. She doesn't fully understand why she stayed. She just did.

## The Factions

Three factions are actively vying for control of the city. They are felt through rumour and small encounters in Arc 1 before the party ever directly engages with them. Brad and his parents operate separately — a fourth, smaller complication, not a faction.

---

### The Purists

**Ideology:** The anomaly corrupted the world. Anything that changed is unnatural and wrong. Charged people are aberrations. The language is purity, natural order, contamination — not religious, but that emotional register.

**Allegory:** Far-right ideological purity movements. The party has powers. The Purists consider them aberrations. The parallel is intentional and should be felt without being stated.

**Structure:** Most organised, most militarised. Former preppers, survivalists, people who already believed the world was going wrong. They had a plan before the anomaly confirmed their fears.

**Breadcrumbs:** Rumour of a settlement that expelled a family because a child developed a power. Whispers about people in uniform moving through certain areas.

---

### The Renewed

**Ideology:** The anomaly was not a disaster — it was a step forward. Charged people are the next phase of humanity. Non-charged are passengers — safe if they contribute, second-class if they don't. Appears warm and benevolent on the surface. Uncomfortable underneath. Idealism tipping into fanaticism.

**Structure:** Looser than the Purists, built around a charismatic leader whose vision holds everything together. People genuinely believe. Non-charged members who question the hierarchy don't last long in positions of influence.

**Breadcrumbs:** A thriving settlement the party hears about — food, water, safety. Sounds appealing. The discomfort surfaces gradually.

---

### The Brokers

**Ideology:** Pure pragmatism. Powers, beliefs, origin — irrelevant. Can you work, trade, fight, build? Then you're welcome. Transactional to the bone. No moral floor — they'll employ anyone and sell anyone out if the price is right.

**Structure:** Economic, not military. They control what commerce still exists. Stalls, markets, supply lines — The Brokers are in the background of all of it. They are the closest thing to a functioning economy in the city.

**Currency:** Marks — small stamped metal fragments, minted by The Brokers. Controlled supply, portable, identifiable. The Brokers effectively function as a central bank. Their marks are the most trusted and widely accepted currency in the city. *"How many marks is that worth?"* The concept of Marks is introduced to the party via their first Broker contact (Arc 1, Ch3).

**Breadcrumbs:** A trader who buys scavenged goods with no questions asked. Later revealed to be one node in a citywide network.
