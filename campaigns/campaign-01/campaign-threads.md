# Campaign Thread Map

This is the co-GM structural document. It maps the major narrative threads across all 41 chapters — not scene-by-scene, but the load-bearing beats, the timing of reveals, and the emotional architecture of the campaign.

**Design principles:**
- Plant early. Pay off late. The slow burn is the point.
- Every Charlotte moment is load-bearing. Flag them always.
- The table runs on banter. The goal is the moments that cut through it.
- Breadcrumbs for Dr. Voss should feel like flavour until suddenly they don't.
- Brad and his family are personal. The factions are political. Dr. Maren Voss is existential. Three different emotional registers — use all three.
- The arc structure is fixed at the chapter 1 and arc closer. Chapters 3–5 of each arc are the most flexible to accommodate player-driven story.

---

## The Five Running Threads

**Thread A — Survival & Community**
Foreground Arc 1, background thereafter. The community centre, feeding people, building something worth protecting. Grounds the stakes in the real and immediate.

**Thread B — Brad & Family**
Seeds Arc 1 (absence only), foreground Arc 2 (Brad), Arc 3 (mother), Arc 4 (father). Personal. These players knew Brad. Some of them probably feel guilty about how they treated him. Use that.

**Thread C — Charlotte**
Introduced Ch2, Arc 1. Rapid attachment built across Ch2–5. The gut punch lands at the Arc 1 closer (Ch6): she attempts Lung Constriction a second time, cannot stop it, slowly suffocates over approximately 30 real-world minutes of play. She dies quietly. After Ch6, she is gone — but her absence is load-bearing. The drawing stays on the wall. Her connection to the party's dormancy is revealed in the final arcs.

**Thread D — The Factions**
Breadcrumbs Arc 1 (mentions, first Broker contact introducing Marks). More active Arc 2 (setup work, showing Brad is unaffiliated). Direct faction storylines Arcs 3–4 (party doing work for each faction to "earn" standing). Faction crisis and climax Arc 5.

**Thread E — Dr. Maren Voss / The Anomaly Truth**
Breadcrumbs Arc 1–4. Surfaces Arc 5. Voss's test subject as Arc 6 boss. Dr. Voss in full for Arc 7.

One player (the mercenary PC) was actively tracking Dr. Voss in the weeks before the anomaly, due to the dangerous nature of her research. They have a file, a name, a face — but no details have been shared with the group yet. They need the party before they can act: witnessing bioelectric powers after the anomaly, they understand they cannot do this alone.

**Why the mercenary can't b-line straight to Dr. Voss:** the anomaly destroyed or scrambled whatever trail they had. The research facility's location isn't fully known — they have fragments, not an address. More critically, they've seen what the research produces. Going in without a strong, capable group is suicide. They are waiting for the right people and the right moment. The party, as they grow in power and cohesion, become that group. This should be an in-world conversation the mercenary PC has with the party at some natural point — ideally after Ch22 when the facility becomes real.

## Arc 1 — Streets of Chaos (Ch1–6, Levels 1–3)

**Arc purpose:** Ground the world. Build the community centre as a home worth having. Introduce Charlotte, build genuine attachment fast, then break them. Everything after this flows from that break.

**Emotional target:** The players should feel the gut punch at the end of Arc 1 with no warning. The chapters before it should feel like setup for a different kind of story. That's the trick.

**Charlotte timeline in Arc 1:** She arrives Ch2. Every chapter after that has at least one small moment with her. The players should feel fond of her by Ch5 without thinking about it. Ch6 is the payoff.

---

**Ch1 — The Supply Run** *(COMPLETE — played 2026-05-02)*
Thread A. Party wakes at Brad's house. Brad absent. Community centre found. Supply run completed. Gang fight on the way back.
- *Seed (B):* Brad is gone. No trace.
- *Seed (E):* Mara mentions people waking up "different."

---

**Ch2 — Charlotte / The Fuel Run**
Threads A + C.

**Charlotte arrives before dawn.** She knocks on the community centre door while it's still dark, before the party head out for the day's job. Small. Quiet. She's been outside for a while — you can tell. She doesn't explain the following. She doesn't understand it herself.

Her parents died in the surge. She watched through the car window. She's been near the party since they left Brad's house. She just... stayed nearby.

Mara takes her in without much ceremony. There's no room for sentiment — there's too much to do. Charlotte sits in a corner. She draws.

**Before the party leave for the day's job:** she approaches one player quietly and holds out a piece of paper. A spell scrap — symbols, precise, drawn from memory. She doesn't know what it is. She drew it from a dream.

**The day's job:** the generator at the community centre is running low on fuel. There's a service station two kilometres out — might still have diesel in the underground tanks if no one's drained them. Getting it out requires equipment and probably a fight.

This chapter is about the fuel run. Charlotte is the margin. But she should feel real in the margin.

- *Load-bearing (C):* She is here. She is real. She drew something she doesn't understand.
- *Seed (E):* The scrap she drew — where does that come from?

---

**Ch3 — The Broker**
Threads A + D.

The party's growing reputation (or simply the need to trade scavenged goods) brings them into contact with a Broker contact — a trader operating out of a converted vehicle or fixed shopfront. Friendly, practical, useful. Pays in **Marks** — small stamped metal fragments.

He explains what Marks are if asked: *"The Brokers mint them. Controlled supply, everyone knows what they're worth. Better than trading tins of beans."* He buys what the party has. He doesn't ask where it came from.

He's also well-informed — perhaps suspiciously so. He knows which streets are contested, which settlements are growing, which groups to avoid. He doesn't offer this as intelligence; it just comes out in conversation.

**Faction seed:** he mentions, neutrally, that a settlement out east turned out a family last month. Kid developed something after the anomaly. *"People get scared. Can't blame them, really."* He doesn't name The Purists. He doesn't have to.

**Charlotte moment:** when the party return, she's helped Mara with something small — stacking, carrying, whatever was needed. Mara gives her a look that isn't quite warmth but is close. Charlotte asks one of the players: *"Where did you go today?"* Simple. She's mapping who comes back.

- *Seed (D):* The Brokers exist. Marks are currency. The Purists exist by implication.

---

**Ch4 — Something Worth Protecting**
Threads A + C.

The community centre faces a direct threat — a competing group eyeing the location, a supply dispute that turns hostile, or an organised attempt to take what Mara's built. The party has to defend it actively.

Charlotte is present during the threat. She is frightened and tries to make herself useful in a way that's more heartbreaking than helpful — she hands someone a scrap of paper (another drawing, another symbol) at exactly the wrong moment, and somehow it turns out to be exactly right.

**This is the chapter where the players stop thinking of the community centre as a place they're staying and start thinking of it as theirs.**

- *Load-bearing (C):* Charlotte in or near danger. The players protect something without being asked. She does something small that matters.
- *Seed (B):* Someone at the community centre mentions hearing Brad's name from a trader passing through. He's alive. He has people with him. That's all.

---

**Ch5 — Further Out**
Threads D + E.

The party ventures beyond their immediate neighbourhood. The world is larger and stranger than their block. Other people have been awake longer. Other groups have built things — some of them impressive, some of them wrong in ways that are hard to name.

A glimpse of The Renewed's territory or reputation — a settlement that's clearly thriving, clearly structured, clearly not entirely comfortable to be in if you pay attention.

Somewhere this chapter: a physical detail that doesn't fit. A symbol on a wall. A torn page with notation that looks like the scrap Charlotte drew. It means nothing yet. File it.

**Charlotte moment:** she's started leaving small things around the community centre — a drawing on the wall, an arrangement of found objects. She's making it hers. One of the players notices. She notices them noticing. Neither says anything.

- *Seed (E):* Someone was studying this before it happened.
- *Seed (D):* The Renewed are out there. They're impressive. They're not quite right.

---

**Ch6 — Arc 1 Closer / The Gut Punch**
Threads A + C. *This is the most important chapter of Arc 1.*

**The chapter has two halves.**

**First half:** A significant threat — the arc boss encounter. Something that requires a real fight and a real decision. The community centre survives. The party wins, or wins enough.

**In the quiet after:** Charlotte brings them something. A drawing — all of them, rough and child-accurate, unmistakably them. She pins it on the wall without saying anything and goes back to her corner.

**The gut punch — Charlotte dies.**

She tried Lung Constriction once before (her first backfire, which the party stopped in Ch4 or Ch5). She thinks she understands it better now. She wants to help. In a quiet moment after the fight, in the relief of having survived, she tries again.

It doesn't stop.

**How to run this (~30 real-world minutes):**
She looks confused first. She drops the scrap but nothing changes. She tries to say something and can't get the words out. She sits down. Her breathing becomes audible — effortful, then laboured. She looks at the nearest player. She is frightened but trying not to show it. She is nine years old and she is trying to act like it's probably fine.

It is not fine.

The party can try anything. None of it works — the spell is internal, bioelectric, not something that can be cut away or countered by force. A high-difficulty Mind roll might slow it, but cannot stop it. The point is not the mechanical outcome. The point is that they try everything and it doesn't matter.

She goes quiet before the end. Not dramatically. Just quieter, and then still.

**The drawing stays on the wall.**

**The electric car:** somewhere in Ch5 or early Ch6 (before the gut punch), the party discovers a working car — simple electric motors, no circuits, unaffected by the anomaly. This becomes their vehicle going forward: modifiable, armable, improvable. It opens the world for Arc 2. Sequence it carefully so it does not overshadow the Charlotte beat.

- *Payoff (C):* Charlotte dies.
- *Bridge to Arc 2 (B):* The party has reason to move. Brad's name becomes serious.
- *New capability:* The electric car expands the world.

## Arc 2 — TBD (Ch7–12, Levels 4–6)

**Arc purpose:** Brad. His absence becomes a presence. The players should spend most of this arc unsure whether he's actually a villain or just a survivor who made hard choices. The reveal should feel earned, not telegraphed.

**Faction role:** Arc 2 does the setup work for the factions — more active mentions, maybe one or two jobs tangentially connected to faction interests, but Brad is not connected to any of them. His independence from the factions is part of what makes him dangerous and interesting.

**Emotional register:** Personal. These players knew Brad. Some of them probably feel guilty. Use that.

---

**Ch7 — A Reason to Move**
Threads A + B. The party are mobile now — the gut punch of Arc 1 has given them purpose. The community centre is still their base, but they're ranging further.

First credible Brad intelligence: someone who met him. A description that sounds right. A camp on the outskirts, organised, disciplined, well-supplied. No aggression reported. Yet.

A Broker contact surfaces again — same one as Ch3, or a different node in the same network. They have work. They mention, in passing, that The Renewed and The Purists have been operating in different parts of the city. Just information.

---

**Ch8 — Broker Job**
Thread D. The Broker contact has a job — retrieval, negotiation, delivery into contested territory. Payment in Marks. Through this job the party sees more of the faction landscape. The Brokers work with everyone. The Renewed are mentioned as good customers. The Purists as difficult but reliable.

The party learns by doing: the factions are real, they're present, and they have reach.

- *Seed (B):* Brad's name comes up again. He's not faction-affiliated. The Broker contact finds that slightly unusual — *"Everyone ends up aligned with someone eventually."*

---

**Ch9 — First Brad Contact** *(Most important chapter of Arc 2)*
Thread B. The party reaches Brad's camp, or Brad reaches them. Either way: he's changed. Harder. Focused. He's built something real — food production, water, scratch-built power. His followers believe in him.

He doesn't act like a villain. He's cautious, controlled, has a story that holds together. He acknowledges the party with something between old familiarity and wariness. He doesn't explain why he left the morning after the anomaly.

**The players must leave this chapter unsure.** Is he fine? Is he dangerous? They don't know. Don't tip it. This chapter requires Brad to be genuinely human.

---

**Ch10 — The Renewed (Proper Introduction)**
Thread D. The party gets a proper introduction to The Renewed — shown around, perhaps invited to speak with the leader. The settlement is genuinely impressive. The discomfort surfaces slowly: non-charged members deferring, being talked over, given different tasks. Small moments of hierarchy made visible.

- *Seed (D):* The Renewed are not straightforwardly good. But they're not straightforwardly bad either. The players should feel the tension without resolution.

---

**Ch11 — Brad's True Shape**
Thread B. Something the party discovers — through investigation or accident — makes Brad's intentions clear. He's not just surviving. He's building toward control. His organisation is strategic, not communal.

Optional: evidence that Brad knew something about the anomaly before it hit, or that his departure from the house that morning was not panic. Enough to raise the question. Not enough to confirm.

---

**Ch12 — Confrontation / Arc 2 Closer**
Thread B.

A real fight. Brad's camp is defended. He knows these players and has spent time preparing.

**The monologue:** when Brad is beaten — physically broken, camp compromised — he talks. This is the most important speech of Arcs 1–4:
- The fury of watching people with powers while he has none
- What he built in the two years they slept — the things he decided, the things he lost
- Something small and specific and true about who he used to be
- The jealousy. Raw. Undignified. Real.

Then he goes — hurt, humiliated, heading to his mother. The players know he has family. This isn't over.

**What the party gains:** Brad's compound. Some followers who stay. A functioning farm, water, scratch-built power. A real base.

---

## Arc 3 — TBD (Ch13–18, Levels 7–9)

**Arc purpose:** Brad's mother. She arrives because her son ran back to her broken and she wants to fix it — which in her case means eliminating the problem. She loves Brad fanatically. She just expresses it by trying to destroy everything that hurt him.

**Faction role:** Arcs 3–4, each faction gives the party storylines — jobs, requests, things they need done — framed as opportunities to earn standing. The party can resolve these positively or negatively. Their choices shape who they're aligned with going into Arc 5.

**Emotional register:** Brad's mother is not a cartoon villain. She's a mother who is absolutely, catastrophically wrong about how to help her son. Understanding how Brad became what he became is part of what makes this arc uncomfortable.

---

**Ch13 — The New Base / Her Arrival**
Threads A + B. The party settles into Brad's compound. New NPCs, new logistics, new problems.

A message arrives — through intermediaries, not directly. She knows what happened. She's coming.

**Charlotte thread:** The drawing is still on the wall at the community centre. Mara keeps it there.

---

**Ch14 — Faction Storyline 1**
Thread D. One faction gives the party a job. Clear deliverable. Marks as payment, plus standing. The party can complete it as asked, find a workaround, or refuse. Every choice has a consequence that carries forward.

---

**Ch15 — Brad's Mother (First Contact)**
Thread B. She's organised and well-resourced. She's found allies — possibly The Purists, whose ideology about charged people aligns with her grief about what "those people" did to her son. She doesn't bluster. She's deliberate.

This chapter establishes her as a threat before direct confrontation.

---

**Ch16 — Research Hint / Faction Storyline 2**
Threads D + E. A second faction job runs alongside the first surfacing of something that points toward pre-anomaly research. A location, a document, a person who knows something they shouldn't.

The mercenary PC's prior knowledge of Dr. Voss becomes relevant here — not fully, but a detail surfaces that they recognise. This is the moment to let that player start connecting dots privately.

---

**Ch17 — Faction Storyline 3**
Thread D. Third faction job. By now the party has done work for at least two of the three factions. The factions are starting to notice each other noticing the party.

---

**Ch18 — Arc 3 Closer**
Thread B. Confrontation with Brad's mother. Cold, purposeful, absolutely certain she is right. She doesn't monologue. She simply acts.

**Aftermath:** Brad and his mother, defeated, flee to his father. The arc closes with the sense that this family is not done with the party — just reorganising.

---

## Arc 4 — TBD (Ch19–24, Levels 10–12)

**Arc purpose:** Brad's father. The quieter, stranger villain. Where the mother was active grief and ferocious protection, the father is contemptuous disinterest — *"why are you so useless"* — directed at Brad, and eventually at the party. He works through proxies. He dismantles rather than attacks.

**Faction role:** Factions are now fully active in the party's story. Jobs become more complex, with conflicting interests. The party's prior choices come back around.

---

**Ch19–21 — Setup, Faction Complications, Father Introduction**
Threads B + D. Brad's father arrives with resources and a longer plan. He's not hunting the party — he's dismantling their support structures through proxies. Community centre, allies, supply lines.

The faction tensions escalate. Two factions may be working against each other in a way that puts the party in the middle. Brad reappears — possibly reluctantly working alongside his father. Still broken. Hasn't healed.

---

**Ch22 — The Research Facility**
Thread E. The party finds the research facility, or a significant portion of it. Damaged. Some things recoverable.

The spell scraps make complete sense here — lab documentation, encoded bioelectric stimulation procedures. Dr. Voss worked here. Their name or title appears on documents. The players find it. They don't get told it.

The mercenary PC has a reaction to something here — a name, a photograph, an ID badge. This is the moment their pre-anomaly knowledge collides with the present. Handle carefully.

This is a lore chapter. Let it be one. The scale of what happened shifts here.

---

**Ch23–24 — Father Confrontation / Arc Closer**
Thread B. Confrontation with Brad's father. Unlike the mother, he's not passionate — he's contemptuous. The party are an inconvenience to be managed. He underestimates them, which is how he loses.

**Aftermath:** The family arc closes. Brad is somewhere out there — broken across three arcs, not dead. His story isn't finished. But it's no longer the main thread.

**Something small:** a moment that echoes Charlotte. Not her directly — something she left, something the party carries, a habit they've developed without noticing. It lands because she's been gone since Ch6 and the players have been carrying her without knowing it.

---

## Arc 5 — The Faction War (Ch25–31, Levels 12–15)

**Arc purpose:** The factions come to a head. The party are now significant players — known to all three factions, having done work for each. Their choices over the previous two arcs determine their relationships and standing. Dr. Voss begins to emerge from the background.

**No single big bad.** The climax of Arc 5 may be the factions tearing each other apart while the party decides whether to intervene, pick a side, or slip away. The outcome depends on player choices. Multiple endings are valid.

**Emotional register:** Political, uncertain, civilisational stakes. The personal arc (Brad's family) is over. The party has to decide what kind of world they want to exist in.

---

**Ch25–27 — Faction War Ignites**
Thread D. Open conflict between at least two of the three factions. The party's prior allegiances are tested. The Brokers are staying carefully neutral and profiting. The Renewed and The Purists may be moving toward direct confrontation. The party has the relationships and the power to tip the balance — if they choose to.

Dr. Voss is pulling strings in the background, using the faction conflict to cover their recovery. Whether the party can see this depends on how much Thread E work they've done.

---

**Ch28–30 — Choices and Consequences**
Thread D. The party's decisions shape the outcome. Factions may survive, collapse, merge, or be destroyed. There is no "correct" answer. The Brokers will survive in some form regardless — they're too adaptable. The Renewed and The Purists are more vulnerable to party action.

**Dr. Voss surfaces** — not fully, not confrontationally, but visibly. The party knows who they are now, or close to it. The question shifts from *what happened* to *what do we do about it.*

---

**Ch31 — Arc 5 Closer**
Thread D + E. Whatever the faction landscape looks like, the party moves past it. The research facility is confirmed as real and reachable. Dr. Voss is recovering. The mercenary PC's knowledge is now fully relevant and shared with the group.

---

## Arc 6 — TBD (Ch32–36, Levels 16–18)

**Arc purpose:** The final approach begins. The research facility in full. The truth of the anomaly. And before Dr. Voss themselves — their assistant and primary test subject, who is the Arc 6 boss.

**The assistant/test subject:** someone who survived Dr. Voss's research with extreme, unstable, catastrophically powerful abilities. They are not evil in the way Brad was evil — they may not have much agency left at all. They are what the research produces at its furthest extent. Fighting them is a preview of what Dr. Voss wanted to create at scale.

---

**Ch32–33 — The March**
Threads E + A. The party prepares and moves toward the facility. What they're leaving behind matters — the community centre, the compound, the people they've built relationships with across 31 chapters. All of it is present as they go.

Player backstories that have emerged should be acknowledged here. Pay off any long-seeded personal threads.

---

**Ch34–35 — Inside the Facility**
Thread E. The full scope of the research. What it was. What the anomaly was meant to do. Who Dr. Voss is, fully named and understood.

Charlotte's connection to everything made explicit if not already pieced together — her dormancy effect, the scraps, the drawing. It all traces back to this place.

The mercenary PC's history with Dr. Voss reaches its emotional peak here.

---

**Ch36 — The Assistant**
Thread E. Dr. Voss's test subject. A fight unlike anything before — not because of raw numbers, but because of what they represent. The full and terrible result of the research, in a person.

Aftermath: Dr. Voss is aware the party is coming. The final arc begins.

---

## Arc 7 — The End (Ch37–41, Levels 18–20)

**Arc purpose:** End it right. The final confrontation. The truth in full. The cost of winning.

**Emotional target:** The players should feel the weight of everything — 40 chapters, everyone they met, everything they built. The ending should feel earned. Not triumphant. Not tragic. Heavy and right.

---

**Ch37–39 — The Final Approach**
Thread E. Dr. Voss at or near full recovered power. Their original goal is close to being achieved — what were they actually trying to do with bioelectric modification of human physiology? The answer should make a terrible kind of sense.

Remaining faction threads resolved. What survives of the city and the people in it.

---

**Ch40 — The Truth**
Thread E. Before the final confrontation: the complete truth of the anomaly, earned through play — not delivered as a dump but pieced together. Why it happened. What it did. What it was supposed to do. What went wrong (or right).

If players have tracked the Thread E breadcrumbs across 40 sessions, this should feel like confirmation, not surprise.

---

**Ch41 — The End**
Dr. Voss. Everything the party has built behind them.

What comes after is up to the table.
