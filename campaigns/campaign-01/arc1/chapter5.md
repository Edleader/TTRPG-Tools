# Arc 1, Chapter 5 — Further Out

## Status: READY TO RUN

## Overview
The morning after Chapter 4. The party range further than they have before — out past the surrounding streets and into the wider district. They find the electric car. They get a proper look at The Renewed's territory and feel its discomfort. They find a fragment that doesn't make sense yet. When they return, the Purist presence around the community centre has tightened.

---

## Section 1 — The Job

Mara has a task that needs doing and it requires going further out than they have before.

> *"Dave — the man with the van — left a note yesterday. Said he has a contact further out who's sitting on a crate of medical hardware. Suture kits, proper antiseptic, blood pressure equipment. That man in the clinic who was infected — he's not getting better. I need what's on that list."*

She pushes a folded note across: Dave's handwriting, a rough location a few suburbs out, and a name — *"Ask for Sully. Tell him you're from Mara."*

> *"It's further than you've gone. I know that. Take whatever you need from the storeroom. And be careful — I've heard things about the area past the park. Some kind of checkpoint."*

She means The Renewed's territory. She doesn't have a name for it.

**Charlotte moment:** She's up early. She has a drawing pinned to the wall — everyone who lives at the community centre, arranged roughly as they sit at the evening meal. It has been there for a day already. She didn't make a point of pinning it. It's just there.

When the party are heading out, she looks up.

> *"They're not going away, are they."*

She means the people outside. Not a question.

---

## Section 2 — The Electric Car

En route to Sully's location, the party passes a residential street with a row of garages. One of the garage doors is partially raised. Visible inside: a small electric car.

It's a basic model — hub motors, minimal circuit boards, the kind of vehicle that was already simplified before the anomaly. Post-surge, it still functions. The key is on a hook inside the garage. The previous occupant is gone. The car has been sitting for a few days — the battery is at about 70%.

> *The interior is clean. Whoever owned it took care of it. There's a half-empty coffee cup in the cupholder, still there from the morning everything changed.*

**Stats:**
- Seats 4 comfortably, 6 with creativity
- City speeds — slow by old standards, but it runs
- Quiet. Significantly quieter than anything else moving in these streets
- Battery range: roughly 80km before needing a charge. Charging requires the community centre generator or a comparable power source.

**The car changes the shape of the campaign.** They can reach further places. They can carry more. They can move faster than people on foot expect. This should feel significant — not as a combat advantage, but as a door opening.

## Section 3 — Out in the World

### The Renewed's Edge

To reach Sully, the party must pass through or near a district that is visibly more organised than anything they've encountered. Cleaner streets. People moving with purpose. A checkpoint — informal but present — where two people ask who they are and where they're going.

**The checkpoint people are friendly:**

> *"Where are you headed? We like to know who's passing through."*

If the party say they're going to find Sully:

> *"Sully's about three streets over. He's fine. Do you have any abilities? Charged abilities, I mean. We're always looking for people with abilities."*

The follow-up question — what happens to people who don't have abilities? — goes unasked. The checkpoint people don't notice the gap.

If allowed through, the settlement beyond is genuinely impressive. Food growing in structured plots. Water access from somewhere — a tank, a pump, a collected supply. People working. It looks like something that will survive.

**The discomfort is subtle:**
- A non-charged resident carrying heavy supplies while a charged person stands talking to a colleague without acknowledging her
- A small sign on a building: *CONTRIBUTION EARNS PLACE* — which sounds reasonable until you think about what it means for someone who cannot contribute in the expected way
- Everything has a hierarchy. The hierarchy is never stated. It is simply there in every interaction.

If the party engage with anyone at length, the warmth is genuine. The structure underneath it is also genuine. Both things are true.

---

### Encounter 1 — The Road Block

Between the checkpoint and Sully's location, a group of three men are blocking the road with a cart. They're not associated with The Renewed — they're opportunists using the quiet of this area to shake down anyone passing through.

> *"Toll. Everyone pays to come through here."*

This is not an official arrangement. The checkpoint people a few streets back would not approve. The men are confident because this usually works.

**They can be:**
- Paid off (they want Marks or food)
- Talked out of it — calling their bluff, pointing out The Renewed's checkpoint is nearby and they'd probably like to know about this (Mind roll, difficulty −2)
- Fought

**Road Block Gang (3):**

| Name | Might | HP | Weapon | Damage |
|------|-------|----|--------|--------|
| Opportunist ×3 | 12 | 20 | Baseball Bat (Simple −1) | 2+d4 |

They do not fight to the death. Two injured and they back off.

---

### Sully

A man in his 50s, running what used to be a small hardware shop. He's converted the storeroom into a supply depot. He confirms Mara's name and produces the crate — suture kits, antiseptic, proper dressings, and a spare blood pressure cuff.

> *"Tell Mara she owes me one. The good kind."*

He doesn't want Marks for this one. He wants the debt. He is not threatening about it — it is just how he operates.

He's been in this area a long time and knows the local shape of things. If the party ask about what's happening around them:

> *"The organised lot? They're good people, mostly. Just a bit — earnest about sorting people. If you're useful to them, you'll be fine. If you're not sure whether you're useful, that's the problem."*

About the people watching the community centre:

> *"Yeah. I've heard about that building. Word gets around. I'd be careful about how visible you make yourselves."*

---

### Something That Doesn't Fit

In an abandoned building near Sully's — a shopfront that still has its signs up, back room intact — a folder of printed documents. Pre-surge. Technical language: *bioelectric frequency response, human subjects, stage 2 monitoring, latency threshold.* Dense notation, like a research paper but formatted like a working document.

On the corner of several pages, a small printed symbol. The same kind of symbol Charlotte draws.

It's not a full reveal. It means nothing yet. Take it. File it.

## Section 4 — Return

### Encounter 2 — The Tail

On the way back to the community centre, the party becomes aware they are being followed. Not aggressively — carefully. Two people, hanging back, stopping when the party stops.

If the party confront them, they look genuinely surprised to be caught.

> *"We just wanted to see where you were going."*

They are young — late teens. One has a notebook. They have been logging the party's movements: time, direction, description. They belong to no faction yet. They are adjacent to the Purists in ideology — charged people are dangerous, they've been told — but haven't fully committed. They are frightened of the party more than hostile to them.

They can be frightened off easily, or reasoned with. They won't fight. If the party take the notebook: it has a list of names and descriptions of people at the community centre, including a rough physical description of Charlotte noted as *"child, unknown ability."*

**GM note:** The intelligence network around the community centre is growing. Someone is compiling information.

---

### The Purists at the Door

When the party return to the community centre, something has changed.

There are people at the end of the street. More than before — six or seven of them, positioned with some deliberateness. White armbands visible now, not tucked away. They are not approaching. They are simply there.

Mara meets the party at the door. She has been watching from the window.

> *"They've been there for two hours. They haven't done anything. They're just — there."*

She pauses.

> *"There were more of them this afternoon than this morning."*

She doesn't say she's frightened. She is frightened.

## End of Chapter

**What the players should feel:**
- The world is bigger than their block. Some of it is impressive. Some of it is wrong.
- They have a car.
- The Purists are here. Not attacking — not yet. But they know this building and they are growing in number.
- Charlotte has made the community centre hers. So, without deciding to, have the players.
