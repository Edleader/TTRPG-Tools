# Last Session Recap (Ch2 — Charlotte / The Fuel Run)

*Read or paraphrase to the table at the start of the next session. Keep it loose.*

## Where we're at
- It's been a couple of days since you woke up. You did the supply run, you fed the centre, you earned a place at the table. Now you're residents, not visitors.
- The community centre runs on a **petrol generator** — it's the only thing keeping the lights on and the hot plates going. Fuel is the whole game.
- Word from Mara at the end of the last night still hangs over everything: **someone nearby is looking for people who came out of the surge "different."** The charged.

## What you did last session

**Before first light — the job**
- **Darren** pulled you aside early: maybe two days of fuel left. He knew a service station near the old train-station end of the suburb with **underground petrol tanks** — if no one had drained them. He handed over a hand pump, a length of hose, and three containers (about 60 litres between you).
- **Mara**, quieter, added a second job on top of the first: *"If there's anything down there worth holding onto, do whatever you can to make sure we're the only ones who know about it."* She left the "how" up to you.

**A kid at the door — Charlotte**
- A knock. **Charlotte** — nine years old, worn shoes, battered backpack, trying very hard to seem unbothered. She'd been near "the house with the dead garden on the corner." That's Brad's house.
- **Mara took her in without a word** — a blanket, a spot by the back wall, a hand through her hair. The first tender thing you've seen Mara do.
- Before you left, Charlotte handed one of you a torn scrap covered in dense, redrawn symbols: *"I drew it from a dream. I don't know what it is. You can have it if you want."* **Cole and Big Donny T recognised it — it's the core of Lung Constriction.** She kept a second copy for herself. *"It's mine. I can always draw it again anyway."*

**The way out**
- Through a long **railway underpass** — about 40m, dim in the middle, a shopping trolley on its side near the far end, funny flat acoustics. *(You'll want to remember this place.)*
- Past that, four people with **white armbands** blocked the footpath. Not a gang — something with more conviction. They clocked that you were from the community centre and let you pass with a warning: *"That place is going to be a problem. Think about where you're putting your loyalties."* They watched you go.
- Spray-painted on a fence nearby, fresh: **UNNATURAL. LEAVE.**

**The service station**
- Big open forecourt, canopy sagging, pumps stripped for copper. Three underground tanks under padlocked caps — **UNLEADED, PREMIUM, DIESEL.** You got the unleaded open and started pumping.
- Halfway through, **five scavengers** came round the back — desperate, improvised weapons, a couple of them smoking. They wanted the fuel. It got messy *(petrol on the concrete, cigarettes, a real fire risk)* — but you came through it and finished the pump.
- Then you dealt with Mara's second job: **hiding / locking off the tanks** so no one else finds the fuel. *(Fill in what they actually did — pushed the wrecked car over a cap, wedged it with rebar, repainted the stencils, etc. This solved the centre's fuel problem for good.)*

**The long way home**
- Loaded down with 60 litres, you came back through the underpass — and the trolley had been **moved**. Someone had been here since morning.
- **Three bikies** were waiting. Big, tattooed, leather, knives out — no armbands, nothing to do with the white-armband crowd. They walked straight at **Big Donny T**, by sight, furious: *"Look who it fucking is."* … *"Where the FUCK have you been."*
- They wouldn't be talked down and wouldn't be intimidated. They wanted Donny dead and didn't care about anyone else. *(The rest of the table just learned Donny has a past.)*

**Back at the centre**
- Darren took the fuel with quiet relief and got straight to filling the generator. When he heard about the tank lockoff, he was impressed. Mara's verdict was one word: *"Good."* (High praise.)
- If you told them about the bikies, both went quiet. Mara: *"Just keep any of your own shit outside of this place."* … then, looking at Donny: *"You all right? All of you?"*
- **Charlotte** ran over when you got back: *"I was beginning to think you weren't going to come back!"* She showed you her latest drawing — more symbols, a different arrangement. She sees them when she closes her eyes.
- The afternoon was quiet and domestic — helping with the generator, lunch, meeting a few residents *(Pat and his dead radio, Iris the nurse, Theo the silent teenager)*, and a **chess game with Charlotte** who plays far better than a nine-year-old should. The suburb went dark in a way it hasn't since you were kids.

**As you wound down for the night:**
> *"Mara's already mentioned, half to herself, that the medical situation is getting tight."*

That's tomorrow's problem.
