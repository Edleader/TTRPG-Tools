# Arc 1, Chapter 2 — Charlotte / The Fuel Run

## Status: READY TO RUN

## Overview
Two things happen this chapter. Charlotte arrives. The party goes out for fuel. These are not equally important. The fuel run is the job. Charlotte is the chapter.

The session opens before dawn — the morning after Chapter 1.

---

## Section 1 — Before First Light

The community centre is quiet. The generator has been running since yesterday and Darren has been watching the gauges. He pulls the party aside early, before most of the residents are awake.

> *"We've got maybe two days of fuel left. After that, no lights, no power for the hot plates. I know where we might be able to get more — there's a service station not far from here, near the old train station end of the suburb. It had underground diesel tanks. If no one's drained them already, there could be hundreds of litres down there. The problem is you need a hand pump and pipe to get at it, and I can't leave Mara to deal with this place alone. Could you go?"*

He hands them a hand pump salvaged from a hardware store and a length of rubber hose. He also has three large plastic containers — they can carry about 60 litres on foot between the group.

**Mara, overhearing:**
> *"Fuel's the only thing keeping the lights on. Without it these people are sitting in the dark. Take whatever you need from the storeroom."*

**Before the party leaves — Charlotte:**

There is a knock at the front door. Darren answers it. He stands there for a moment, then calls for Mara without saying anything.

**Charlotte** is outside. She is small — nine years old, though she carries it reluctantly, the kind of kid who tries to seem unbothered by things that are clearly bothering her. Her shoes are wrong for the amount of walking she's done. She has a battered notebook tucked under one arm.

She doesn't explain herself immediately. If the party ask who she is:

> *"I'm Charlotte."*

If asked where she came from:

> *"I was near the house. The one with the dead garden on the corner."*

That's Brad's house. She's been in the area since the morning after the anomaly — weeks, possibly. She doesn't offer this. If pressed on how long she's been outside:

> *"Not that long."*

She has been outside all night.

**What Mara does:** takes her in without ceremony. She finds Charlotte a blanket and a spot near the back wall, then goes back to what she was doing. This is not coldness. This is someone who has already absorbed a great deal and keeps moving.

**What Charlotte does:** sits in her corner. Pulls out the notebook. Draws.

---

### The First Scrap

Before the party head out, Charlotte approaches whoever seems least intimidating. She holds out a torn piece of paper without making eye contact.

It's covered in symbols — dense, precise, unlike anything the party has seen. The symbols are arranged in a pattern that has clearly been drawn and redrawn many times. This one is a clean copy.

> *"I drew it from a dream. I don't know what it is. You can have it if you want."*

She goes back to her corner. She does not explain further.

**GM note:** This is a functional spell scrap — Lung Constriction. If two players already have this spell, they will recognise it immediately. Charlotte has a second copy in her notebook that she is keeping.

**If the party recognise the scrap and try to take the one Charlotte is keeping:**
She will scrunch the copy in her hand, pull it to her chest, and say:
> *"It's mine."*

If they push further, she looks at them steadily:
> *"I can always draw it again anyway."*

She means it. She sees the symbols when she closes her eyes. Do not push this further — it is hers.

**What she doesn't say:** her parents died in the anomaly surge. She watched through the car window. She has been near this group since they left the house on the corner — quietly, at a distance. She doesn't fully understand why she stayed. She just did.

## Section 2 — The Fuel Run

The service station is roughly two kilometres away. The streets are quiet this time of morning but not empty.

---

### Encounter 1 — The Patrol

About halfway to the station, the party moves through a stretch of road that passes a row of shops with boarded windows. A group of four people emerge from a side street ahead — they are walking with purpose, and when they spot the party, they spread out to block the footpath.

They are not a gang. They are something with more conviction than that. They have white armbands. The one in front is in his 30s — composed, not aggressive, but not moving.

> *"Where are you headed this early?"*

If the party say they're on a supply run:
> *"From the community centre?"*

He knows the community centre. He doesn't elaborate. If the party confirm:
> *"Just so you know — that place is going to be a problem. You might want to think about where you're putting your loyalties."*

He lets them pass. He watches them go.

**GM note:** This is the first direct Purist contact. They are not attacking. They are identifying people and logging what they see. Do not give them a name yet. Do not explain further. File it.

---

### Scene — Something Painted on a Wall

On the footpath side of a fence, in rough spray paint: **UNNATURAL. LEAVE.**

It's recent — the paint hasn't fully weathered. No one is around.

The party can look at it and move on. Do not explain it. Just make it visible.

---

### Scene — The Service Station

The forecourt is intact — the canopy still standing, though a couple of the pump housings have been pulled open and stripped for parts. The access cap for the underground tank is in the forecourt floor — locked with a padlock.

**The lock:** Bolt cutters will cut it cleanly (Might roll, difficulty −2). A careful approach to the mechanism (Finesse roll, difficulty −2) can pick the access cap. The padlock is rusted — a determined player can force it with the right tool.

**Once opened:** the tank has diesel. Not full, but substantial. The hand pump works with some effort. Pumping 60 litres takes about 15–20 minutes.

---

### Encounter 2 — The Scavengers

Halfway through the pumping, two people arrive at the forecourt. They came from the direction of the train station — they saw the party heading here and figured out what they were doing.

They are not organised. They are desperate. They have improvised weapons.

> *"We need that. Step back."*

They don't want a fight — they want the fuel. If the party show they won't back down, the scavengers will hesitate. They can be negotiated with (offer to share a container), intimidated (Might roll, difficulty −2), or fought.

If it goes to a fight, they retreat at the first serious injury rather than fight to the death. They were hoping for easy pickings, not a brawl.

**Scavengers (2):**
- Might 11, 15 HP each
- **Kitchen Knife:** Simple −1, 1+d4 damage
- **Crowbar (one of them):** Simple −1, 2+d4 damage, reach 1m

**Possible complication:** the pump jams mid-fight. A Might roll (difficulty −1) or a creative mechanical solution fixes it. Not dangerous — just a clock.

## Section 3 — Return

The walk back is quieter. 60 litres of diesel in containers is heavy work.

Darren receives the fuel with something close to relief, though he doesn't perform it. He gets straight to filling the generator.

**Charlotte moment:** when the party come back, Charlotte is helping Mara stack tins — quietly, without being asked. She hears them come in and doesn't look up immediately. Then:

> *"Did you find it?"*

Whoever answers, she nods. Goes back to what she was doing.

**If anyone asks what she's drawing:** she shows them. More symbols — different arrangement, same kind of dense precision. She says she sees them when she closes her eyes. She doesn't know what they mean. She seems unbothered by this in the way a child is unbothered by things they don't have context for yet.

## End of Chapter

**What the players should feel:**
- The generator problem is solved, at least for now.
- There is a child here now. She draws strange things. She doesn't ask for much.
- Someone is watching the community centre. Someone painted a wall.

**What not to explain:**
- Why Charlotte has been following them.
- What the symbols mean.
- Who the white-armband people are.
- Who painted the wall.
