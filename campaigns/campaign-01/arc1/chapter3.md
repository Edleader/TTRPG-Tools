# Arc 1, Chapter 3 — The Broker / Wider World

## Overview
The morning after Chapter 2. The world gets bigger. Mara has a concrete medical run for the party — supplies for someone running a fever — and a vague map of where to look. While they're out, they encounter their first **Broker** and learn about **Marks**, witness the early shape of something watching them, find a fragment that doesn't quite fit, and run into two very different problems on the way out and the way home. The session ends with the day's haul delivered and one or two new threads opened.

**Combat encounters:** the Hollow Man and his dogs (an unhinged charged man on the approach to the chemist) and the Opportunists (a small ambush on the way home, by people who saw what the party had).

---

## Section 1 — The Morning Problem

The party wake to the centre stirring around them. Whoever is up first sees Darren on the front step in the cold, watching the street like he's listening for something. Inside, Mara is already moving — checking the food stocks, looking in on the older residents, marking something off a list she keeps in her head.

She catches the party before they've finished waking up properly.

> *"I need medical supplies. Specifically — antiseptic, wound dressings, and if you can find it, anything for infection. We've got one of the older residents running a fever. It's probably nothing."*

A beat.

> *"I'm not equipped for it if it isn't."*

She unfolds a hand-drawn map on the table — the same kind of rough, growing-as-she-learns map you'd expect from someone who's been piecing the local geography together from descriptions for weeks.

> *"There was a chemist on the high street, and a medical clinic around the corner from it. I don't know what state either is in. Someone told me there's also a man with a van who trades — apparently he's been set up near what used to be the small shopping strip for a few days now. Might be worth talking to him."*

She looks at the party, neither pleading nor demanding. She has done this enough times that she knows asking is a separate skill from running a centre.

> *"I'd go myself, but I can't leave this place. Just bring back what I've asked for. Anything else you find is yours to decide on."*

**Charlotte moment — before the party leave:**

Charlotte is sitting near the door with her notebook on her knees. She looks up at whoever is the last to gear up.

> *"Where are you going today?"*

Whoever answers, she nods, and goes back to drawing. **She is mapping who comes back.** Don't draw attention to it — just play it as a small ritual.

## Section 2 — Out in the World

The high street is roughly fifteen minutes' walk from the centre, through streets the party are now starting to know. The morning is overcast — bright but not warm. The suburb is more awake than yesterday morning, but still in patches.

> *"The high street is what you'd expect. Half the shopfronts boarded over by the people who tried to defend them, the other half stripped open by people who didn't have to. Glass on the footpath in places. Some shops have been picked clean and left; others are sealed up tight and the silence around them suggests there might still be people inside. You don't slow down to find out."*
>
> *"The chemist sits on a corner about halfway down. You can see it from a block away — the green cross logo above the door, slightly crooked, half its tubes still intact behind smashed glass. The shopfront window is gone. The door is propped open with a brick."*

### Encounter 1 — The Hollow Man and the Dogs

**Setup:** the encounter happens **on the approach to the chemist** — within sight of the green cross sign, but not at the door yet. The party round a corner, or come across the high street, or step into an alley as a shortcut, and into the encounter. **Choose the geography that fits how your players are moving.**

**Read or paraphrase as the party approach:**

> *"You hear them before you see them. A low, throaty growling — not one dog, several. It's coming from somewhere off the high street, toward an alley running between the chemist and the next building over. Then a man's voice, talking, low and constant, like he's having a conversation that doesn't need an answer."*
>
> *"You round the corner."*
>
> *"There's a man sitting on the lid of a wheelie bin halfway down the alley. He's filthy. Skinny. His hair's a mess and he hasn't shaved in weeks. He's wearing what used to be office clothes — a pale shirt that's gone grey, suit trousers torn at one knee. He's holding a half-eaten can of something and talking to it. He might be talking to you. It's not clear."*
>
> *"Around him are four dogs."*
>
> *"They are big. Two look like staffies, one looks like a kelpie crossed with something heavier, and the fourth is a thin grey thing whose ribs you can count. They are not on leads. They are arranged around him in a rough half-circle, watching the alley mouth. Watching you."*
>
> *"He looks up. He smiles. It is not a kind smile. It is the smile of a man who has made friends with the world finally."*
>
> > *"It's all right. They're with me. They're all right with me."*
>
> *"The dogs do not look all right."*

**The situation, GM-side:**

The man — call him **The Hollow Man** internally; he doesn't introduce himself — was charged by the anomaly. Not strongly. Not the way a Voss test subject was. Just enough that something in him reaches out **passively, without his knowledge, and quiets the part of nearby animals that would otherwise be afraid of him.**

He believes the dogs simply love him. He has been alone in this alley for weeks with them. He talks to them. He shares food when he has it. He has, in his own small broken way, **found a family.**

He is also dangerously unstable. Whatever his life was before the surge — wife, job, mortgage, kids — none of it survived the awakening. He is running on grief and hunger and a quiet certainty that the dogs are the only things in the world that understand him. **He will not let the party take any of his food, his alley, or his dogs.**

**The dogs themselves:**
- Are not magically controlled. They are **real, hungry, half-feral animals** whose fear of humans has been suppressed by a passive bioelectric effect they do not understand. They follow him because in his presence they feel safe; they would, in any other context, be running scared.
- Will **attack on his cue** — even unspoken. If he becomes agitated, they pace and growl. If he panics or feels threatened, they lunge.
- **Will stop** if he is unconscious, dead, or removed from the alley. Without him, the suppression fails — within a round or two they are back to being scared, hungry strays. They will scatter. They will not pursue.

**How the encounter can play:**

This is **a fight with a question inside it.** Run it that way. The party should feel that the man is not evil — he is broken, and the world made him this way. Give them every opportunity to **resolve it without killing him.**

**Possible approaches:**
- **Talk to him.** A Mind roll (difficulty −1) reads that he is not entirely present. He responds to questions in fragments — *"They were here when I woke up. They didn't run. Nothing else didn't run."* Gentle voices land better than loud ones. **A successful Mind roll lets a player notice the dogs' eyes flicker — not on the man, but on each other — when calm voices are used.** Something in them is being held in place.
- **Offer food.** If the party offer the dogs food — actual food, not a gesture — the dogs will hesitate. So will he. He will accept, eventually, if the party are patient. **He will not let them take his can.**
- **Threaten / draw weapons.** The dogs lunge immediately. He stays sitting on the bin and watches. He may or may not pick up his broken bottle. He is not the threat. They are.
- **Try to incapacitate him without harming the dogs.** A grappling Might roll, or a Mind/charged-power approach (Static Discharge, etc.). **If he is knocked unconscious, the dogs whine, mill in confusion for a round, and scatter.** Fight ends.

**If it goes to a fight:**

**The Dogs (4):**
- **Might 12, Finesse 12, 12 HP each.** Roll under 12 to hit.
- **Bite:** 1+d4 damage. Pack-tactic: a dog gets +1 to hit if another dog is engaged with the same target.
- They go for the closest moving target. They will commit fully — they are not strategic, just hungry and unafraid for the first time in a long while.

**The Hollow Man (1):**
- **Might 10, Finesse 11, Mind 14, 18 HP.**
- **Improvised weapon (broken bottle / kitchen knife):** 1+d4 damage. He is not a fighter. He swings if cornered.
- He does not move from the bin unless physically forced to. **He is the source of the encounter, not the front line of it.**
- **If knocked unconscious or removed from the alley:** the dogs disengage within 1–2 rounds. Fight effectively over.
- **If killed:** the dogs disengage immediately and scatter. The party will likely feel something about having killed him. Don't comment on it. Let them feel it.

**Aftermath:**

- **If they spared him:** what to do with him is open. They could leave him in the alley (he'll likely die within weeks). They could try to bring him to the centre (Mara would accept him; he would be very difficult). **Don't railroad — let the players decide.** If they bring him in, he is named, fed, and quietly disturbing in the background of Ch4 onward. He may become useful or tragic. We'll decide based on what the players want him to be.
- **If they killed him:** the body stays in the alley. The dogs are gone. The party may notice the food can he was eating from is half a tin of canned peaches, and he was sharing it with the dogs. **A small, ugly detail. Don't dwell — but don't hide it either.**
- **Either way:** somewhere on his person is a faded photo, soft from being carried — a woman and two children, smiling at a barbecue. He will not have spoken about them. **Charged people are not always heroes. Some of them are this.**

**GM note:** this is the first time the party have witnessed **uncontrolled charged ability in someone unhinged.** Don't explain it. The dogs' unnatural calm in his presence is the only real cue. If a player asks how the dogs are behaving like that, *"You're not sure"* is the right answer. **File it.** This is a Thread E echo, not a payoff.

### Scene — The Chemist

> *"The chemist's shopfront has a smashed window — looting happened early. Most of the front shelves are stripped. Empty boxes, scattered packaging, the tang of some long-evaporated topical cream still hanging in the air. Whoever did the front of the shop was in a hurry and didn't know what was worth taking."*
>
> *"The back is a different story. A frosted-glass partition separates the dispensary from the front. The door is locked from the customer side, but the lock is a cheap retail catch — a hard kick or a determined shoulder will go through it. Behind the partition, the dispensary is small and tidy and largely intact. Shelves of labelled boxes still organised alphabetically. A locked glass cabinet at the back. A computer terminal with its monitor smashed but the keyboard still neatly aligned. A medical poster on the wall about handwashing, faded. A kettle. A mug with a teaspoon still in it."*
>
> *"It's the kind of room that feels like someone left and never came back."*

**What's here:**
- **Antiseptic** — several intact bottles. Easily enough for what Mara needs.
- **Wound dressings** — full sealed packs, a satisfying haul.
- **Disposable gloves** — a box, half-full.
- **Basic pain relief** — paracetamol, ibuprofen, several intact blister packs.
- **In the locked glass cabinet:** a sealed pack of **broad-spectrum antibiotics.** This is the prize. Finesse roll, difficulty −1, to pick the cabinet lock. Or break the glass — but the noise carries.
- **Behind the counter, a notepad** — prescription notes from before the surge, which a curious player might leaf through. Most of it is mundane. **One entry near the back is just symbols** — the same dense pattern as Charlotte's drawings. Not a prescription. Just a single line, traced over multiple times like the writer was thinking. *Charlotte was not the only person who saw these. Don't draw attention. File it.*

**The medic — Hana.**

There is someone already in the dispensary when the party enter.

> *"A young woman — mid-20s, medical scrubs that have clearly been worn for several days too long, dark hair tied back, a face that has not slept properly. She is sitting on the floor with her back to the locked cabinet. She has heard you coming for at least a minute. She has a kitchen knife on the floor next to her, within reach, but she has not picked it up."*
>
> > *"Don't shoot. I'm not — I'm not anyone."*

She introduces herself as **Hana.** She was a nurse — second-year, finishing her shift at the clinic around the corner when the surge hit. She walked here in the immediate aftermath because the chemist felt safer than the clinic. She has been here **most of the time since**, surviving on scavenged tinned food and what's in the dispensary kettle.

**What she'll tell the party (if they're friendly):**
- The clinic around the corner is structurally intact. She doesn't want to go back there. *"There were people in there last week. They left. I don't know if they're back."*
- There's a man with a van a couple of streets over. *"He came past once, shouted hello, didn't come in. He paid someone for bandages with little metal coins. I saw it from the window. I don't know what they were."*
- People have been **moving through the area at night.** She doesn't know who. *"They don't make noise. They're just there and then not there."*
- **About the alley:** if the party mention the Hollow Man, her face does something — *"He's been there for weeks. The dogs. I don't go that way anymore."* She didn't know what to do about him. She felt guilty about it.

**What she will and won't do:**
- **Will not** leave with the party today. She's been alone for weeks. The idea of moving feels enormous.
- **Will** accept supplies — food, batteries, a candle. Won't ask for them. Will be visibly grateful.
- **Will** tell the party about the centre's location if asked. *"I might come find you. One day. If things get worse."*
- **Will** notice if a party member has medical knowledge (Cole especially). Asks careful, professional questions. Looks at him with the relief of someone who hasn't talked shop in months.

**GM note (kept light, do not overcommit):** Hana is a **future option**, not a commitment. She may come to the centre in Ch4 or Ch5. She may not. She may stay at the chemist and become a known place to come back to. **Decide based on player attachment.** Do not front-load her — the party should not feel like she is "owed" to them. Just a person, surviving.

### Encounter 2 (non-combat by default) — The Lookouts

**Setup:** between the chemist and the shopping strip where the broker's van is parked, a few hundred metres of open street.

**Read or paraphrase:**

> *"Two people are standing at a street corner doing nothing. One of them is leaning against a power pole. The other is looking at the ground. As you pass, the one against the pole looks up — not at you, but past you, like he's checking who's behind you. The one looking at the ground takes out a small notebook and writes something in it without looking down at the page."*
>
> *"They are not visibly armed. They are not aggressive. They are also clearly not just standing there for no reason."*

**If the party confront them:**

> *"We're not doing anything."*

If the party push:

> *"You're the group from the community centre, aren't you. We've heard about you."*

He says it like a fact, not a threat. He looks pleased to have confirmed something. They walk away when the conversation finishes — not running, not nervous, just *done*. **One of them, when his sleeve rides up, has a small white symbol — a stylised circle — on the inside of his collar.**

**GM note:** the party are being **logged.** The Purists have informants throughout the area. This is not a fight — it's a feeling. **They are visible in a way they weren't before.** Do not name the Purists yet. Do not explain the symbol. File it.

**If the party push for a fight:**
- The first two will not engage. Two more emerge from a side street. Now there are four.
- They still won't throw the first punch.
- **If forced into combat:** Watchers (4): Might 12, 18 HP each. Improvised weapon (pipe / bottle): 1+d4 damage. Roll under 12. **They disengage and withdraw the moment any of them takes meaningful damage.** They are not here to fight today. **They report back. The party have just made themselves a problem.**

If you do run this as combat, **don't replace it with the planned outbound or return fight.** This becomes a third combat — a hard session — so only do it if the players genuinely demand it.

### The Broker — Dave

**Setup:** the old shopping strip is two streets further on. A row of small shops, all stripped or sealed. **In one of the parking bays, a converted Mercedes panel van** — sliding side panel open, makeshift counter clipped to the inside, three or four organised shelves visible behind it, a folding chair set out. The van itself is rough but cared for. The shelves are not.

> *"A man is sitting in the folding chair in the sun, eating a tinned peach with a spoon. He is in his forties. Faded denim jacket, sun-creased face, a small radio that doesn't work clipped to his belt out of habit. He sees you coming a long way off and does not move. He chews. He swallows. He waves."*
>
> > *"Morning. You buying or selling?"*

He introduces himself by first name only — **"Dave"** — and has clearly been doing this for a few days. He buys and sells.

**He pays in Marks.**

If the party haven't heard of Marks, he pulls a small canvas pouch from a pocket and tips a few onto the counter — **small flat metal stamps, irregular but minted, with a stylised B punched into one face.**

> *"Brokers mint them. Whole network of us. Controlled supply, everyone knows what they're worth. Bag of beans is one thing today, three next month — these aren't. Better than trading by the can every time you want something."*

**He's well-informed in the way that comes from talking to everyone who passes through.** If the party engage:

- **Streets:** he'll tell them which corners are quiet and which to avoid. Won't say *why*, just tone of voice.
- **The Hollow Man (if asked):** *"Yeah, the dog bloke. Heard about him. Wasn't going down that alley either."* He says it with respect for the dogs, not the man.
- **The expelled family rumour:** mentions, neutrally — *"Settlement out east turned out a family last week. Kid developed something after the anomaly. One of the charged ones. They were asked to leave."* He says it like weather. *"People get scared. Can't blame them, I suppose."* **He does not name any group.** He doesn't know the name, or doesn't use it.
- **The watchers (if asked):** *"Yeah, I've seen them. Tracking something. Wouldn't worry about it."* He clearly would worry about it.
- **The community centre (if asked):** *"Mara's place? Yeah, good outfit. Tell her hello from me if you want — she'll know what for."* He won't elaborate.

**What he'll buy:** scavenged tools, food surplus, anything from the chemist they want to part with, useful finds. He gives **fair, slightly low** Marks for things — explains the prices honestly: *"Demand for that's not great right now. Best I can do."*

**What he'll sell at reasonable Marks cost:**
- Rope (good condition)
- Duct tape (full roll)
- A working lighter (luxury)
- Basic first aid kit (smaller than the chemist haul, but compact)
- Candles (a bundle of six)
- A hand tool or two — pliers, a flathead screwdriver
- **A small clay bottle of strong alcohol** — not for drinking. *"If you've got a wound and no antiseptic. Or if you want to set something on fire."*

**He has no weapons.** *"Not really my thing."*

**GM note:** Dave is a **Broker node**, useful, personable, and has no moral floor. He is not a villain. He is just not constrained by anything other than profit. Both things are true simultaneously. He **reports back** — by the time the party leave the high street, the Brokers as a network have a more detailed picture of who they are. The party will not feel this immediately.

**Optional: Dave gives them a small bag of starter Marks.**
> *"You're new. I'll front you a few. Spend them or don't. Either way you'll know what they feel like."*
This is a deliberate move — establishes goodwill, gets them invested in Marks as a system, and lets them feel rich for ten minutes. **Five Marks each player.** Track this.

### Scene — Something That Doesn't Fit

**Setup:** on the way between Dave's van and the medical clinic — or while looking around the chemist if the party are observant — one of the players notices something.

**Where:** a wall near the medical clinic, or the corner of a metal shopfront shutter, or the back of a brick at the side of an alley. **Pick the spot that fits where the players are looking.**

> *"Etched into the metal — or scratched into the brick, depending on where — is a small symbol. About the size of a coin. Not spray paint. Cut in carefully with something sharp. Whoever made it took their time."*
>
> *"It is dense. Precise. Made of small shapes packed together in a pattern."*
>
> *"It looks exactly like something Charlotte has drawn."*

**GM note:** this is the **second occurrence of Thread E.** Charlotte's symbols exist outside her notebook. Someone with a steady hand and time has been marking them in the world.

**Don't draw attention to it.** Just place it in the scene. If the players notice and ask: *"You don't know what it is. No one else seems to."* If they sketch it / take a photo with the imaginary phones they don't have / try to remember the location — **bank that.** The location may matter later (it doesn't yet).

**Optional, if the party are very observant:** there is a **second** symbol in the area, maybe ten metres away from the first, identical. **Whoever did this did it more than once.** Don't drop both unless the party are searching.

### Scene — The Clinic

> *"The medical clinic is a single-storey brick building, a converted house with a wheelchair ramp added at some point in the last decade. The sign above the door reads HIGH STREET FAMILY MEDICAL — partly faded, otherwise intact. The front door is closed but unlocked. A faint smell of stale air and something more medical underneath as you push it open."*
>
> *"Inside: a small waiting area with mismatched chairs and a coffee table covered in dust. A reception counter with a sliding glass partition still mostly closed. Two examination rooms off the main corridor, doors ajar. A small storeroom at the back. A child's drawing has been pinned to the noticeboard for at least two years and is starting to curl at the corners."*

**What's here:**
- Additional dressings, a blood pressure cuff, a working pulse oximeter (battery-operated — **may or may not work** depending on how the GM wants charged objects to behave).
- More antiseptic than the chemist had.
- In the storeroom, an unopened **case of saline bags.** Heavy, but valuable.
- A wall-mounted defibrillator — dead, but the **physical pads and gel** can be salvaged.
- **Behind the reception desk, on a clipboard:** a list of patient appointments scheduled for the day after the anomaly. None of them ever happened. Whoever wants to read it can. *Pick out one detail to read aloud — a name, a time, a reason for visit. Make it human. "10:15 — Mrs Thom. Follow-up re: hip."*

**The two people inside:**

In the waiting area: an older man and a teenage boy, sitting on the chairs. The older man's right arm is wrapped in a poorly-tied bandage that has gone through and is leaking yellow. He looks up when the party enter. He does not stand. The boy stands, half-protective, half-ready to run.

> *"We didn't take anything. We've been here three days. We didn't take anything."*

They are **not hostile.** The older man is the boy's grandfather — they were caught out away from their place when the man's arm got infected, and the boy didn't know where else to go. The clinic was the only place that felt safe.

**The arm:** badly infected. **Cole — or any character with medical inclination — recognises it immediately.** Without antibiotics, the man may not last another week. With the antibiotics the party just took from the chemist, he has a real chance. **A Finesse roll, difficulty −1, or simply careful description of treatment, gets the wound cleaned and properly dressed.**

> *"You're medics, then?"* — the old man, when the party start working on him.

**What they tell the party (if helped):**

- They live a few blocks away — the boy describes a small flat above a closed laundromat. Quiet. Not safe to go back to right now.
- A few weeks ago, **people came through the area looking around.** Not the white-armband ones. Different. They had a man with them whose name the boy didn't catch. He was *organised* — gave instructions, the others listened, they didn't take anything but they looked at everything. Then they left. **The boy thinks the man was injured but trying to hide it.**
- This is the **first vague hint of Brad.** Don't push it. Don't have them describe Brad in detail. The boy doesn't know the name. The old man wasn't paying attention. **Just plant the impression.**

**If the party leave them alive and help them:**
- They will accept directions to the centre and may turn up there in the next day or two. They are **not faction-affiliated**, just survivors. Mara will take them in. They become low-stakes background residents.

**If the party ignore them or leave them be:**
- They remain at the clinic. They will likely die. **The party will not necessarily learn this.** Their fate is bankable — pull on it later if it suits.

## Section 3 — The Long Way Home

The walk back is heavier — saline bags are real weight, plus everything from the chemist. The streets are warming up; the afternoon's first sunlight is breaking through cloud. The suburb is fully awake now, and the party are visible — they're carrying things people want.

### Encounter 3 — The Opportunists

**Setup:** the party are roughly halfway between the clinic and the centre, on a stretch of street they came in on. **A small detail can plant earlier:** at the chemist or the clinic, Hana or the boy mentioned *"there've been people watching the place. They don't come close."* **If you remembered to plant that, the ambush feels earned.** If not, no problem — narrate it anyway.

**Read or paraphrase:**

> *"You're walking past a row of small shops — boarded windows, peeling paint, a dry-cleaner's sign hanging by one chain. Three people step out from behind a parked car about fifteen metres ahead of you. Not running — moving quick but trying to look casual, like they've practised this in their heads."*
>
> *"They are **not** the white-armband people. They are not organised. They are wearing what they were wearing two years ago when the anomaly hit, faded and stretched and patched. They look hungry. One of them is sweating in a way the morning doesn't account for."*

**The lead one — older, mid-30s — speaks. He's holding a blade that doesn't fit the rest of him:** a short, clean, beautifully balanced knife, the kind of thing that was expensive once. Everything else about these three is patched and worn and scavenged; the blade is the exception. And unlike the other two, he *knows how to hold it* — it sits in his grip easy and ready, turning a slow idle circle as he talks. Whoever he was before, he can use it. It catches the morning light.

> *(GM: draw the eye to the blade deliberately — it is the one thing on any of them worth having, and the party should clock that. He is the dangerous one precisely because he can wield it; watching him land a clean hit with it is what makes the party want it. It is the reward for this encounter.)*

> *"You picked up a lot of stuff. We've been watching that chemist for a week — couldn't get past the dog bloke. You did. Fair play. Now we need some of what you've got."*

He's trying to sound casual. He isn't. His eyes keep flicking back to the sweating one — the sick one — like he's checking on him.

**The situation, GM-side:**

- These three have been **camped near the high street for days,** maybe weeks. They watched the chemist. They watched the Hollow Man and his dogs. They couldn't get past either.
- **One of them is sick.** Some kind of infection — not catastrophic yet, but worsening. The lead one is his brother (or partner — whatever lands). They came **specifically because they saw the antibiotics in the party's haul** and they need them.
- **They are not killers.** They are scared people who have decided that today is the day they take a risk because the alternative is watching someone close to them die.
- **They will fight, but only just enough.** If the lead one goes down hard, the others retreat fast. If the sick one is incapacitated or threatened openly, the lead one will negotiate.

**Possible approaches for the party:**

- **Fight straight up.** Stat block below. Resolves quickly — they're not seasoned.
- **Negotiate.** A Mind roll (difficulty −1) reads quickly that the sweating one is sick. **If the party offer to share — antibiotics, dressings, anything — the lead one's posture changes immediately.** *"You're serious."* They will accept and back off without a fight, possibly with awkward gratitude. They will not come back for more.
- **Intimidate.** A Might roll (difficulty −1), backed by the visible haul they're carrying, is enough to make the three of them reconsider. They retreat, glaring. **The sick one looks at the party as he goes — that look will stay with the table.**
- **Kill them all.** Possible. Easy enough. **The body of the sick one has a small framed photo in his jacket pocket — him and a young woman, smiling, on a beach. Recent enough that it must have been taken just before the anomaly.**

**Stat block (if it goes to a fight):**

**The Opportunists (3):**
- The two knife-wielders have **15-18 HP, Might 11-12, Finesse 11-12.** Roll under 12 to hit.
- **Lead one — balanced short blade:** 1+d6 damage, melee. **Finesse 15 — rolls under 14 to hit. He is skilled with it and genuinely dangerous;** the party will feel the difference between him and the other two. **Will retreat if reduced below half HP.** *The blade is the loot the party wants — and watching him use it well is the reason they want it. See "The blade" note below.*
- **Knife (×1):** 1+d4 damage, melee.
- **Knife (sick one):** 1+d4 damage, melee. **Has disadvantage on attack rolls** — hands shaking. Sweating. Fights badly. **15 HP, gives up the moment he's hit.**

**Aftermath:**

- **If the party shared:** the three retreat with the dressings/antibiotics they were given and disappear up a side street. **They may surface again** — bankable as future allies or future complication.
- **If the party fought and won:** bodies. The party are now visibly carrying medical supplies they took violently. **No one in the centre needs to know.**
- **If the party let one or more flee:** the survivors carry the story of what the party did. Word travels in this kind of suburb. **The party's reputation is now slightly more concrete — fair, generous, brutal, dangerous, depending on what happened.** Bank it.

**The blade (loot):** whether the party fight, intimidate, or negotiate, make the **Balanced Short Blade** available to them.
- **If they fought:** it's on the lead one — dropped, or prised from his hand. Obvious.
- **If they intimidated:** as the three back off, a clever party member can demand the blade as the price of letting them go. The lead one hesitates, then tosses it at their feet — it clearly wasn't his to begin with.
- **If they negotiated / shared supplies:** the lead one offers the blade in return, almost embarrassed. *"Here. Take this. It's not… it was never really ours."* A quiet admission that they took it off someone too.
- **Mechanically:** it's a fine Finesse blade — Simple (−1), 1+d6, one hand — but it only rewards a **Finesse 15+** wielder with the reduced difficulty. Right now that's **only Fat Tony.** Hand it out via the DM card-give tool. If Tony isn't present this session, the party can still carry it back for him.

**GM note:** this fight is **deliberately the inverse** of the Hollow Man encounter. The Hollow Man was unhinged and pitiable; the Opportunists are sane and pitiable. **Different shapes of broken.** The party should leave today aware that the world is full of people doing terrible things for reasons that almost make sense.

## Section 4 — Return

The walk the rest of the way back is short. The mood depends on what the party did out there.

**Mara takes the haul without ceremony.** She inspects the antibiotics, nods, says *"Good"* — the same single syllable as yesterday, meaning the same thing. She immediately turns to brief Iris (or whichever resident is helping her) on dosing for the feverish patient. *That patient — never named on the page, kept generic — improves visibly within an hour. By the end of the afternoon, their fever is breaking.*

**Charlotte moment:** she's been folding donated clothes, or stacking something small. When the party come in, she has a new drawing ready. She slides it across the table to whoever sits nearest — more symbols, different arrangement.

> *"You came back."*

Not a question. Just a thing she noticed. **She marks something in her notebook.**

**If the party tell Mara about the Hollow Man:**
She listens. Doesn't say much. *"There's more like him out there than people realise. Most of them don't have the dogs."* She is implying something. She doesn't elaborate.

**If the party tell Mara about the Opportunists:**
She is quiet for a moment.
> *"What did you do?"*
She is not judging. She wants to know. **Whatever the answer, she absorbs it.** She doesn't comment further. **But she remembers.** The party will catch her watching them slightly differently for the rest of the day.

**If the party brought back Hana, the old man, the boy, or anyone else:**
She is briefly frustrated — *"I've got room for so many"* — and then immediately practical. She gets them settled. This is what she does.

## Section 5 — The Afternoon

> *"By the time everything's been sorted and the antibiotics are in the right hands, the day's tilted past noon. The centre has the same middle-of-the-day quiet you remember from yesterday. You've got the rest of the day."*

**GM note — same structure as Ch2.** The afternoon is a **menu of options.** Pick whatever fits time and player energy. **If the session is running out of time, skip to the Montage Fallback.**

**Mood:** the centre feels slightly more like home than it did yesterday. People nod when they see the party. Names are starting to attach to faces. The fuel is keeping the lights on; the medicine is keeping someone alive in the back room. **Whatever the party did out there today, it landed.**

---

### Scene A — The Patient

The feverish resident — pick a generic resident, don't tie to specifics — is on a cot near the back, breathing easier now that the antibiotics have been administered. They are sleeping for the first time in two days.

**A player who comes near:** they may want to check on them. A simple, quiet scene. **Cole especially.** No rolls — just the experience of having helped someone they didn't know yesterday. The patient may wake briefly, see the player, mumble thanks, and drift back. **Quiet earned moment. Don't drag it.**

---

### Scene B — Marks

The party have starter Marks (if Dave gave them any). The centre has a small **internal trade going on** — residents swapping small things they brought with them or scavenged. A pair of socks for a shave from someone with the only working razor. A book for an hour's babysitting. **Marks aren't really being used yet** — but the moment they appear, residents are interested.

**A player who pulls out their Marks:** suddenly half the residents have something they'd consider trading. Pat would trade a story for one. Someone else has a bar of real soap they've been hoarding. **The party can spend Marks here on small comforts** — a clean shirt, a bowl of something better than the stew, a deck of cards.

**GM note:** this is **the introduction of the economy.** Marks are now a real thing. This scene establishes it. **Don't track too tightly** — let the players feel rich, then hint that they're going to need more soon.

---

### Scene C — Word From Elsewhere

A new face is at the centre when the party return — **a man who walked in mid-morning** while they were out. Mid-50s, wiry, walked here from a few suburbs over because he heard there was somewhere organised. He was let in after a short conversation with Mara.

He is full of news. Most of it fragmentary, second-hand, half-rumour. Sit a player or two next to him and let him talk. **Things he might mention, pick one or two:**
- A settlement out east is **growing fast** and **taking everyone in.** Free food, free shelter, no questions. Sounds too good. *Renewed seed — don't name them.*
- A different group, **in uniform-like clothes**, has been searching houses in his suburb for *something*. He doesn't know what. They didn't take anything from his place. They asked questions. *Purist seed, more direct than the watchers.*
- **A man with a camp.** He doesn't know the name. He heard from someone who heard from someone. *"Said his people were the ones to be with if you wanted things to actually get better. Couldn't tell you any more than that."* *Brad seed — keep vague.*
- A **convoy of working vehicles** was seen on the highway last week. **Working** — which means someone has fuel, mechanics, and organisation.
- The **rumour about the expelled family** circulates here too. Same story, different mouth.

**GM note:** this scene is **breadcrumb infrastructure.** Pick one or two pieces — don't dump them all at once. They are seeds for Ch4, Ch5, and Arc 2. **The old man eventually fades into the background of the centre.** Name him if a player asks; he's nobody important.

---

### Scene D — The Drawing on the Wall

Late in the afternoon, **Charlotte has done something new.** Whoever notices first sees it — she has tacked a drawing to the wall near her usual corner. It is bigger than the scraps she usually does. Not symbols this time — a **picture.**

**It is the community centre.** From the outside. The shape of the building, the door, the boarded windows, the gas lanterns lit. It is unmistakeably this place, drawn carefully, in soft pencil.

She has drawn each of the **adult residents she's noticed** standing at the door — small, recognisable figures. **The party are at the front.** Mara behind them. Darren off to one side.

She doesn't say anything about it. If asked: *"I just wanted to do one of everyone."*

**GM note:** this is the **Charlotte attachment beat for Ch3.** She is starting to make this place hers. **The drawing stays on the wall.** It is the same wall the Ch6 drawing will eventually replace. **Don't draw attention to it again later** — but it's there.

## End of Chapter

**What the players should feel:**
- The world is **bigger** and **more textured** than yesterday — there's an economy, a faction starting to be visible, news from further afield, charged people who aren't them.
- **Charged ability is not always heroic** — the Hollow Man was a glimpse of what it can do to someone broken.
- **Other people are watching them now.** The centre has a reputation, and it isn't only good.
- **The medicine kept someone alive.** That is the win of the day.

**What not to explain:**
- What the symbols mean.
- Who the white-armband people are or what the small white circle means.
- Why the dogs were calm around the Hollow Man.
- Who the man "with a camp" is.
- The full reach of the Brokers.
